## 3. Say less. Say it safely. (1:30) · 3 steps
Health ads are reviewed closely, and every claim is checked before it runs. The slider moves from risky to safer wording.
- **Step 1, outcome promises.** "Stops cancer from coming back" is a promise. I would say: "Ayurvedic supportive care, guided by experienced doctors."
- **Step 2, cure language.** "Treats the root cause of cancer" is cure language. Safer: "A holistic approach to wellbeing during cancer care."
- **Step 3, replacing treatment.** "A natural alternative to your treatment" tells a patient to skip care. Safer: "Can be taken alongside your ongoing treatment. Consult your oncologist."

This wording is illustrative. Every claim needs medical and legal review before it is used.

**Transition:** With the account protected, here are nine fixes, in priority order.

## 4. Nine fixes, in priority order (2:30) · 3 steps
- **Step 1 (01–03).** Fix tracking first: form fills, WhatsApp taps and calls as separate conversions, because the real result is a counseling call or an appointment, not a click. Then respond faster, with tagged leads so the platform learns who actually books. Then one page per need, so ad and page match.
- **Step 2 (04–06).** Win local search with an accurate Google Business Profile for each branch. Put the main budget on high-intent search. Educate and build trust with doctor-led content in Telugu, Hindi and English, with no outcome promises.
- **Step 3 (07–09).** Respect privacy: no condition-based audiences or remarketing, checked against current platform policy. Speak their language, with Telugu and Hindi tests. And report in one view: a weekly dashboard with cost per qualified lead. This is where my engineering background helps most.

## Questions they may ask (draft answers: edit to match what is true for you)

**Why move from AI engineering to ad campaigns?**
Ad performance is a measurement and systems problem as much as a creative one: tracking, automation and reporting decide whether spend can be trusted. That is the work I already do. I want to apply it where the stakes for families are real, and where honest communication matters most.

**How would you handle ad claims in cancer care?**
No cure or outcome claims, ever. I would write a compliance checklist and an approved-phrases library in the first 30 days, and nothing would run without medical and legal review. No condition-based audiences or remarketing, and I would check everything against current platform policy. Policy flags are a KPI, and the target is none.

**How would you measure success in 90 days?**
First month: record a baseline. Then track cost per qualified lead, lead-to-appointment rate, appointment-to-visit rate, cost per visit, landing page conversion and policy flags. Numeric targets are set with your team after the baseline, so they are realistic. At day 90 I would present a review and a next-quarter plan.

**What would you do first?**
Audit the accounts, tracking, landing pages and Google Business Profile, and record baselines. In the same weeks, fix conversion tracking so calls, WhatsApp taps and forms are counted separately, and meet the counselor, medical and legal teams to learn the approval process.
